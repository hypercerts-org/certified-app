The deciding insight

An org is a full account, not a namespace

Two kinds of "org" run through these patterns, and they take opposite models.

Namespace-orgs → per-action scope

A GitHub org or a Medium publication can't be logged into. They are containers you attach records to. Per-action scoping (Option 3) fits because there is no account to "be".

Account-orgs → act-as / delegation

An atproto org is its own account: its own DID, handle, repo, profile, follow graph. You could log into Bluesky as Acme directly. X and YouTube brand accounts work the same way, and both use "operate the account", not a per-action tag.

A Certified org is the second kind. So Option 3, by framing the org as an owner you tag onto a record, hides what the org actually is and breaks consistency with the rest of atproto. The act-as model tells the truth: the org is an account you operate. And doing it in-app is delegation, which beats "log in as Acme" directly: no credential sharing, you stay accountable as the operator, and you never log out of yourself. That delegation gap is exactly what raw atproto lacks and Certified can fill.

Suggestion

Act-as, framed as delegation

Keep the act-as model (it is the honest one for an account-org and it bridges to Bluesky), but treat the safety layer as mandatory, not optional. One line states who you are operating and who you are:

AC
Operating Acme Collective as @holke (admin) Switch to personal
1 · Persistent indicator
That bar on every screen, with the inline exit. It names the org (the account) and you (the operator) at once.
2 · Confirm the high-stakes write
Endorsing as the org gets a confirm naming endorser, operator, and subject. Following is one click.
3 · Session-scoped, not forever
The mode lasts the session, not indefinitely in localStorage, so you cannot be silently delegated days later.

Why not Option 3: it is safest by construction, but it buys that safety by pretending the org is a namespace. For an atproto identity product, telling the truth that the org is a real account matters more, and the mis-attribution risk is answerable with the three conditions above. The org-endorse backend (group badge route + per-group endorsement definition + unfollow route) is needed either way, so it is not a reason to prefer one model over the other.
